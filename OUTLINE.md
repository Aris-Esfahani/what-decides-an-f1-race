**Part 1: Explore (Level 1\)**  
 *Goal: describe what’s happened, using charts and tables.*

* 1.1 How often does pole position turn into a win, by driver and by decade?  
* 1.2 How have retirements (crashes, mechanical failures) changed since the 1950s?  
* 1.3 Which teams dominated each era?  
* 1.4 Grid position vs. finish: scatter plots by decade.  
* Output: 4–6 charts, each with a 2–3 sentence takeaway.

**Part 2: Test claims (Level 2\)**  
 *Goal: check whether the patterns from Part 1 are real or just chance.*

* 2.1 Does grid position matter more at Monaco than at other tracks?  
* 2.2 Have pit stops gotten meaningfully faster, beyond fractions of a second?  
* 2.3 Is there a real home-race advantage?  
* 2.4 Track specialists: do some drivers beat their expected finish at certain tracks?  
* For each one, write: the claim, the test you used, the result, and what it means.  
* Output: a findings table showing which claims held up and which didn’t.

**Part 3: Predict (Level 3\)**  
 *Goal: use Parts 1–2 to build a model, then test it on races it hasn’t seen.*

* 3.1 Choose the prediction target: podium (yes/no), finishing position, or finishing the race at all.  
* 3.2 Features, each justified by a finding from Parts 1–2:  
  * grid position  
  * team strength  
  * recent form  
  * track type  
  * home race  
  * track-specialist score  
* 3.3 Train on, e.g., 2010–2022 and test on 2023–2026.  
* 3.4 Compare against baselines like “finish \= grid” or “pole sitter wins.”  
* 3.5 Check which features mattered most.  
* Output: model accuracy vs. baseline, a feature-importance chart, and where the model fails.
